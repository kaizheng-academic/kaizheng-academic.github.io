Problem Setting

RNA-targeted discovery is constrained by sparse supervision: in real screening pipelines, most RNA-ligand pairs are untested rather than truly negative. This creates the Unknown-as-Negative (UaN) bias, where unlabeled positives contaminate the negative set.

Limitation of Naive ERM

Under UaN contamination, standard empirical risk minimization may optimize toward a biased surrogate objective. This bias can force representation collapse in local neighborhoods of active scaffolds and damage manifold continuity of structure-activity relationships.

TRACE Theoretical Pillars

  1. Confidence-weighted importance sampling to rectify surrogate risk under posterior drift.
  2. Thermodynamic temperature regulation to stabilize gradients and constrain local geometry.
  3. Margin-aware contrastive regularization to separate true binders from decoys with explicit probabilistic guarantees.

Guarantees and Impact

TRACE combines statistical guarantees with practical screening utility: stronger out-of-distribution generalization, improved robustness under heavy noise, and substantially better calibration for wet-lab prioritization.
